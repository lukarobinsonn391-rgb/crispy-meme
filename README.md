<h2><strong>Mistake 1: Preparing for the Old GRE</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The GRE changed in a big way on September 22, 2023. ETS shortened the General Test from about 3 hours 45 minutes to about 1 hour 58 minutes.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It now has one 30-minute &ldquo;Analyze an Issue&rdquo; writing task, two Verbal Reasoning sections (12 and 15 questions) and two Quantitative Reasoning sections (12 and 15 questions).&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Older books, forum posts and courses may still describe the longer test, an &ldquo;Analyze an Argument&rdquo; essay, or longer sections.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That mismatch can wreck your pacing. You may practice at a speed or stamina level the real test doesn&rsquo;t ask for, or train on a task that no longer appears.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 2: Skipping the Official Practice Tests</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Third-party questions can sharpen your skills, but only ETS writes the real test. ETS offers several official practice options on its POWERPREP page:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>POWERPREP Test Preview Tool:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> free, and it familiarizes you with question types, test features and help tools.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>POWERPREP Online:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> two practice tests that simulate the real test experience, available through your ETS account.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>POWERPREP PLUS Online:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> three additional official practice tests, each with scores and explanations, sold separately. They were listed at $39.95 each when I checked.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">ETS&rsquo;s student workshop guidance suggests using the first POWERPREP test early as a baseline and the second near the end to measure progress.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many students do the reverse, burning through official tests too early or saving them all for the last week.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Take an official test first to find your starting point, then use the others to check progress and rehearse test day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The official prep books and services page lists the Official Guide and the Verbal and Quant practice question books.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 3: Treating the First Section of Each Measure as a Warm-Up</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The GRE is section-level adaptive. ETS says the difficulty of your second Verbal or Quantitative section depends on how well you did on the first one in that measure.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you coast through section one, you may hand yourself an easier second section, which can limit your ceiling.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This catches students who save their focus for &ldquo;the hard part&rdquo; later.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On the GRE, the first section of each measure helps decide what the second section looks like.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Practice treating the first Verbal and first Quant section as scoring sections. Warm up before the test, not during it.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many prep guides also recommend tracking accuracy on your first sections in practice tests so you can see how it affects your second ones.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 4: Leaving Questions Blank</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">ETS states there is no penalty for unanswered or incorrect answers on the GRE.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A blank answer can only cost you points. A guess gives you a chance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Students sometimes freeze on a hard question, run low on time, and leave several answers empty. That is a pure loss.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Answer every question before time runs out. The GRE lets you skip around within a section, mark questions, and change answers as many times as you like before you leave the section. Use that freedom:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Answer the questions you know quickly.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mark tough ones and move on.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Return to marked questions with the time you saved.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the last minute, fill in any remaining blanks.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You can read more about these test features on ETS&rsquo;s structure page and in its GRE vs. GMAT guide.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 5: Memorizing Vocabulary Without Context</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vocabulary matters on the GRE, but a long word list is a weak way to learn it. The Verbal section includes reading comprehension, text completion and sentence equivalence.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These questions test how words work in a sentence and passage, not just what they mean in a dictionary.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Students who memorize hundreds of words but never practice reading dense passages often struggle with speed and accuracy.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learn words from the questions you miss and from real reading.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Practice with sentences and passages, then review why each wrong choice fails.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Read longer academic and nonfiction articles regularly to build stamina.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mix vocabulary study with reading comprehension practice, instead of treating them as separate projects.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>Mistake 6: Rushing Past Math Fundamentals</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GRE math is not advanced, but it is tricky. It tests arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis, and it expects you to read questions carefully and use shortcuts wisely.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Students who jump straight into hard practice sets often miss basics that cost easy points.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">ETS publishes free math review materials and a &ldquo;Math Conventions&rdquo; document that explains the assumptions, notation and terminology used in GRE questions.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skipping it can cause avoidable errors, such as misreading a figure or assuming something the test does not state.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Review fundamentals first, read the Math Conventions, and practice with the on-screen calculator you&rsquo;ll see on test day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The GRE provides a calculator in the Quantitative Reasoning sections, so learn when it helps and when mental math is faster.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 7: Ignoring Analytical Writing</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Every GRE begins with the 30-minute Analyze an Issue task, scored from 0 to 6. Because Verbal and Quant scores get more attention, many students skip writing practice entirely.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That can backfire if a program you&rsquo;re applying to looks at your writing score, or if you freeze up on the first task and carry stress into the rest of the test.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Practice at least a few timed essays. Plan for about five minutes, write for about twenty, and leave a few minutes to revise.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">ETS lists online writing practice among its official prep tools. Ask a friend or tutor to review your essays, or compare them to the scoring guidelines.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 8: Only Practicing in Short, Untimed Bursts</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Doing 20 questions at a time at your own pace builds skill, but it doesn&rsquo;t build test-day stamina.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The real exam asks for nearly two hours of focused work with a time limit on each section.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Students who never take full-length timed tests often run out of time or lose focus late in the exam.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Schedule several full-length, timed practice tests, ideally at the same time of day as your real appointment.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Simulate test conditions: no phone, no pauses, and the same kind of screen you&rsquo;ll use. Official POWERPREP tests are ideal here because they mimic the real interface.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 9: Reviewing Mistakes Too Quickly</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Finishing a practice set and glancing at the right answers is one of the most common time wasters in test prep.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You can do a thousand questions and learn very little if you don&rsquo;t figure out why you missed them.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Keep an error log. For every wrong answer, note:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The question type (for example, quantitative comparison or text completion)</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Why you missed it (a knowledge gap, a careless mistake, a timing issue, or a misread)</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">What you&rsquo;ll do differently next time</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">After a week or two, patterns show up. You may notice you miss certain geometry topics, rush text completions, or fall for the same trap answers.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Then you can target your study time where it counts.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 10: Collecting Too Many Resources or Picking by Hype</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some students buy three courses, four books and two apps, then feel overwhelmed and finish none of them. Others choose a resource only because it is popular on forums.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A better plan is simple: one main course or structured plan, plus official ETS materials for practice tests and real questions.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Compare options on how they teach, what they cost, the format of their practice, and whether they&rsquo;ve been updated for the shorter GRE.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Mistake 11: Planning the Test Date Carelessly</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Booking the GRE too early or too late both create problems.</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Too early:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> You test before you&rsquo;re ready and burn a fee. ETS raised the GRE General Test fee to $249 for registrations on or after August 1, 2026, up from $220. ETS also lists a Fee Reduction Program for eligible test takers, who pay $100.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Too late:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> You leave no room for a retake before your application deadlines.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Retake rules matter here. The GRE allows you to test once every 21 days, with a maximum of five attempts in a rolling 12-month period, according to GMAC&rsquo;s comparison and ETS&rsquo;s registration rules. Check the current ETS GRE Information Bulletin for the latest policy.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Work backward from your earliest application deadline. Leave time for your first attempt, a possible retake, and score delivery.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Confirm each school&rsquo;s score requirements and published medians, since ETS does not publish a GRE-to-GMAT conversion and schools use their own benchmarks.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Also look into ScoreSelect, which lets you choose which scores to send to schools after you test.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Mistake 12: Letting Test Day Surprise You</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Even strong students lose points to avoidable test-day problems: a late arrival, an ID mismatch, or a setup issue when testing at home.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Small stresses add up and can pull your focus from the questions.</span></p>
<p><strong>What to do instead:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Decide early whether you&rsquo;ll test at home or at a test center, and read the rules for each.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Check ID requirements and your exact name match well before test day.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Do a technology check if you test at home.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sleep well, eat beforehand, and plan to arrive early.</span></li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>Frequently Asked Questions</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>What is the biggest GRE prep mistake?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The most costly mistake is practicing with materials that don&rsquo;t match the current test, especially studying for the old longer format. Check dates, and use official ETS practice tests to confirm you&rsquo;re training for the real thing.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Is there a penalty for wrong answers on the GRE?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">No. ETS states there is no penalty for unanswered or incorrect answers, so you should answer every question.</span></p>
<h3><strong>How many practice tests should I take?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There is no magic number, but most students benefit from several full-length, timed tests, including the official POWERPREP tests. Use the first as a baseline and the later ones to track progress and build stamina.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Does the first section of Verbal or Quant matter more?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On the GRE, how well you do on the first section of a measure affects the difficulty of the second section in that same measure, so give both sections your full effort.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Can I retake the GRE if I make a mistake?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yes. You can test once every 21 days, up to five times in a rolling 12-month period, but each attempt costs a fee, so a solid plan beats repeated tries.</span></p>
<h3><strong>Should I skip the Analytical Writing section prep?</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&rsquo;s better not to. The writing task comes first on test day, and some programs look at the writing score. A few timed practice essays are a low-cost way to avoid a shaky start.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Final Thoughts</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Strong GRE results come less from studying harder and more from studying smarter.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Match your prep to the current test, lean on official ETS materials, treat every section seriously, answer every question, and review your errors until they stop repeating.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fix these common mistakes early, and the points you gain will come from skills you built, not luck on test day.</span></p>
